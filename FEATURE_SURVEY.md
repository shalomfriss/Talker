# AAC and autism app feature survey

Research snapshot: 2026-11-01. This is a growing inventory of published product features. “Every autism app” has no fixed boundary: app stores and product versions change, and many useful AAC apps are not marketed only for autism. Each row links to a publisher or App Store description. Descriptions are paraphrased; licensed vocabularies, symbols, and page sets are not copied into Open Voice.

The [ALS Association's communication-board collection](https://www.als.org/communication-boards) and [partner-assisted spelling board](https://www.als.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/Partner%20Assisted%20Scanning%20Spelling%20and%20Phrases%20Board%20BW%20%281%29.pdf) document printable alphabet boards, number choices, and prompts such as Wait and Please guess. Open Voice's speech-cue PDF gives its on-screen letter and topic cues a paper version. Its on-screen Partner mode scans highlighted English or Spanish letter, number, and action rows through yes/no confirmation to build a spoken or partner-facing message. Space, Delete, Wait, and Please guess can be selected through the scan; communicators and partners agree on yes/no signals first.

The expanded optional page library follows [ASHA guidance on inclusive, personally meaningful core and fringe vocabulary](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/augmentative-and-alternative-communication/) and [AssistiveWare guidance on accessible personal fringe words and a keyboard](https://www.assistiveware.com/learn-aac/learn-about-core-word-teaching-strategies). Its seven new English/Spanish pages cover home, shopping, friends and family, hobbies, sensory needs, technology, and rights and choices; users can edit the added buttons. The phrases and emoji cues are original Open Voice content.

## Research method and limits

[ARASAAC’s published terms](https://arasaac.org/terms-of-use) limit pictogram use in products to non-profit purposes and require source/author credit and share-alike licensing. Direct in-app ARASAAC search and import remain open while distribution rights are unresolved; Open Voice can still import images a user already has rights to use through Photos or Files.

The inventory uses publisher pages and App Store listings found through targeted searches for AAC, autism communication, visual schedules, social stories, and alternative access. A broader census would need repeated searches by country, language, platform, and store category, followed by hands-on verification of each app version. Store descriptions are claims by developers, and some advanced features depend on paid editions, supported devices, or additional content.

The communication passport uses editable, user-chosen topics because [CALL Scotland's Communication Passports guidance](https://www.communicationpassports.org.uk/creating-passports/gathering-information/) says the owner should choose what matters to share. Its support and boundary fields also reflect [AssistiveWare's AAC supporter checklist](https://download.assistiveware.com/aac/1.3-remove-barriers-to-communication-checklist.pdf).

The communication-repair page offers phrases for asking for time, correcting a message, and keeping control of a conversation. A direct Speak and large-key keyboard control also speaks these phrases without changing either draft. This choice follows [AssistiveWare's communication-partner guidance](https://www.assistiveware.com/learn-aac/build-communication-partner-skills) on waiting for an AAC user to compose and finish a message; the page content is original and editable.

[AssistiveWare’s grammar guidance](https://www.assistiveware.com/learn-aac/teach-grammar) describes word endings as one way to expand AAC expression. Open Voice now offers English and limited Spanish picture-word and typed-word form pickers with audible preview; this does not cover sentence agreement or broad conjugation.

Choice boards complement the full vocabulary rather than replacing it. [AssistiveWare's guidance](https://www.assistiveware.com/learn-aac/roadblock-stalled-at-choice-making-and-requesting) cautions against limiting AAC to choices and requests, and [Communication Matters](https://www.communicationmatters.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/aac_services_standard_aug_2012.pdf) includes the ability to reject offered choices among communication rights.

[TouchChat Gateway’s vocabulary manual](https://touchchatapp.com/assets/uploads/Gateway_Introductory_Manual-2015-01-21.pdf) describes the Modified Fitzgerald color groups used for people, verbs, little words, describing words, things, and places. Open Voice offers a per-profile border or soft-fill version; social words use a neutral color. Personal button colors and high contrast take priority.

The optional 1–10 pain scale and specific-area choices follow [NHS guidance on communicating pain in autistic healthcare appointments](https://www.leicspart.nhs.uk/autism-space/health/preparing-for-health-care-appointments/), which recommends adapting pain scales to individual needs. Speaking pain remains possible without choosing a location or number.
